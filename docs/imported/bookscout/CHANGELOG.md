# Changelog

## 0.6.3

- **A calculator for each payment.** Open a payment on the Payments tab to see what every collaborator is owed from it (each line split by the percentages for its sales month, after tax), with CSV and a PDF per author. **Allocate** a payment to freeze those amounts: a later change of split no longer rewrites them, and the app tells you when a split has changed since.
- **Pay authors: the quarterly roll-up.** A new tab shows, for a quarter (or month, year or range), each author's unpaid balance from before, what Amazon paid in the period, what you have already paid out, and what is due now. Record each payout you make and the balance carries forward. Payments dated in the future are not counted until they are received. CSV and a payout statement (PDF) per author.

## 0.6.2

- **Author statements from what Amazon actually paid.** Import the KDP **Payments** report (new **Payments** tab). Amazon pays one lump sum in USD, two months or more after the sales, after tax, so each payment line (a sales month and Sales or Kindle+) is matched to your royalty reports. Statements now default to **Paid by Amazon in the period** (by payment date), split by each line's sales month and the split for that month, on the net after the tax Amazon withheld, in USD. **Earned in the period** (by sales month) is the other basis: paid sales are owed, unpaid ones show as estimates that are not owed yet. Each line says whether it is paid (with payment number and date), awaiting payment, or not checked. CSV and PDF carry the payment, the tax and the unpaid lines.
- **Payments tab.** Every payment with its lines, tax, net and the bank conversion (pending until the bank reports it), a line-by-line check against the royalty reports (including months Amazon revised after paying and months with no royalty report), and what is earned but not paid yet, by currency.
- **Kindle+ rates are exact for paid months** (taken from the payment's Kindle+ lines), and "Kindle Unlimited" is now called **Kindle+** in the app, as KDP calls it.
- Without a payment report statements work as before and are marked as not checked against a payment. A payment line in a currency other than USD (not seen yet) has its USD value worked out from the payment's total and is marked as such.

## 0.6.1

- **Royalty splits find the authors for you.** The authors on each book (the names KDP reports, and the authors credited on captured pages) are offered when you set a split, so nobody has to be added first. Picking a name creates the person; typing a name still works as a backup. A collaboration's book is pre-filled with its authors at equal shares to adjust. If one real person writes under several pen names, add them under People with those pen names and every book under any of them is paid to that person, split or not.
- **Royalties and splits: open a book on Amazon to capture its authors.** Each book in the split table has an **Open to capture** button (and the split editor has one too), which opens the book's Amazon page ready to send with the extension. A KDP report names only one author of a collaboration; once the page arrives, every author credited on it is offered in the split.

## 0.6.0

- **My Books: Charts and analysis, a Month view, and Royalties and splits.** My Books now has four tabs. *Charts and analysis* shows royalties by month (eBook, print, Kindle Unlimited), units and pages per day, every book's earnings, and breakdowns by marketplace, royalty rate and kind of sale. *Month* shows one month in full against the month before and the same month a year earlier. *Royalties and splits* lets you add the people who are paid, give each book a split (one split per book; changing it asks from which month it applies), and produce an author statement for a month, quarter, year or custom range, saved as CSV or PDF. Statements include eBook, print and Kindle Unlimited royalties, convert other currencies to USD at an editable monthly rate, and leave out the current month's Kindle Unlimited until the month is over.
- **The KDP workbook's royalty sheets and Summary are imported.** Royalties per book, marketplace, royalty rate and currency were never read before. Kindle Unlimited money is worked out from the Summary (its royalty minus the royalty sheets') once KDP has finalized a month, and estimated from the last known rate until then. A paperback listed under its own ASIN is treated as the same book as the eBook with the same title, and no longer adds a second row to My Books.
- **Fix: a monthly "Orders Processed" sheet was read as one-day totals.** In KDP's workbook that sheet is by month, so a month's sales showed up as a single day (the 1st). It is now skipped and the daily Orders Placed sheet is used. Your saved reports are read again the first time this version opens.
- **Fix: KENP Read reports headed "Kindle Edition Normalized Page (KENP) Read" were not recognised.**

## 0.5.2

They now work. A new test checks that every function the app declares is on the bridge, so one cannot be left off again.

## 0.5.1

- **Reader Community: "What is being discussed" lists search terms, not filler.** The phrases table counted any word that recurred across threads, so "world", "without", "already", "next", "don't", "back", "keep", "kind" and "help" topped it. A topic is now a term from the genre's vocabulary (creatures, settings, jobs, tropes and moods, including single words such as "romance", "magic", "shop" and "stakes"), or a pair of meaningful words that recur together. A lone recurring word that is not in the vocabulary, and filler (pronouns, auxiliaries, prepositions, adverbs, light verbs, and reader chatter such as "character", "genre", "cover", "vibe", "little"), is never a topic. Terms show as a reader would search them ("dragons"), and the category's own words are still left out.
- **Keyword suggestions are search terms, not recurring words.** Suggestions and the seven strings used to include any word that recurred in the listed books' blurbs, so filler such as "next", "don't", "back", "behind", "must" and "become" got through. Words that recur are no longer suggested on their own. A suggestion now comes from the readers' requests, the genre's tropes, a vocabulary of what readers search for (creatures, settings, jobs and moods: "dragons", "cooking", "low stakes", "strong female lead", "magic system", "talking animal", "magical romance", "closed door", "spicy", "romantasy") that the listed books' titles and blurbs carry, or from a phrase recurring in titles whose words are all meaningful (pronouns, auxiliaries, prepositions, adverbs and light verbs are rejected). The seven strings are two or three words that the same books carry together ("dragons cooking"), at most four.
- **Library: category cards open their Amazon page and show when they were last captured.** The category name on each card at the top of the Library is a link that opens that category's Best Sellers page on Amazon, ready to capture again. Each card also shows the date of the last list capture and the days since.
- **Keywords: Suggest Keywords now builds seven keyword strings for KDP.** The five single words were the wrong shape: KDP's Keywords section has seven boxes of up to 50 characters, and Amazon matches the words in a box both singly and together, so a box should hold a few related terms a reader would search. Under the suggested phrases, **Seven keyword strings for KDP** lists seven (fewer, and it says so, when the category's books do not hold enough distinct terms), each with its length against 50 and a Copy button; hover one to see why each term is in it. They follow what KDP and the authors who test it agree on: no word appears in more than one string, none repeats a word of the category (or of the book picked under **Avoid words in**: its title, subtitle and blurb), stop words and filler ("to", "and", "book", "kindle") are dropped, and so are words KDP does not allow in keywords ("best", "bestselling", "free", "new", "award"). Terms readers ask for in the synced subreddits come first, then the tropes and recurring title phrases of the listed books, then phrases that autocomplete offered for the category's keyword research; terms that the same listed books carry are put in the same string, and a phrase autocomplete lists ranks above one it answered empty. Nothing is requested to build them. Available in the app as `keywordStrings`.

## 0.5.0

- **Keywords belong to a category.** The Keywords tab has one **Category** drop-down (it starts on your first category; "All categories" files nothing). A phrase you search, and the terms of a Google Trends CSV you import, are filed under the chosen category; Search History lists the phrases researched for it, with a Category column; the Reddit column reads its subreddits, and Suggest Keywords suggests for it (the separate drop-downs are gone). The answers themselves are still shared by phrase, so searching the same phrase under two categories asks nothing twice. Searches made before this are not filed anywhere and show under "All categories"; searching one again under a category files it. Data packs carry these links, so an exported category brings its keyword research (autocomplete answers, history, Trends) filed under that category; research that was never filed is still included when an Amazon search of it shows mostly the category's books or the phrase contains the category's own phrase, and arrives filed under it.
- **Data packs: export and import a category's data.** Settings has a new **Share data** card. **Export a data pack…** saves, for everything or for the categories you tick, the pages BookScout has captured (book pages, bestseller lists and covers), the Amazon searches whose results are mostly those categories' books (at least 20% and two), the linked subreddits with their stored text, the keyword research filed under the category, and the books you removed from the category. That is enough to open the category's analysis on another computer. **Import a data pack…** adds a pack to your library: pages you already have are kept and importing the same pack twice changes nothing. Each page is read again by the current parsers, and a damaged or unexpected entry is skipped. Book text, samples, notes, the queue, My Books and sales figures are never in a pack, and the search index is rebuilt after import. A keyword answer already in the library is kept when it is newer than the pack's.
- **"Subreddit not found, check the spelling", before the sync starts.** The first sync of a subreddit now begins with one request asking Arctic Shift for the subreddit's newest post. If the archive has no post from it at all, the sync stops there (no seven days of requests) and says so in red; it can also mean the subreddit is private, banned or too new for the archive. A check that fails or times out does not block the sync, and a subreddit that already has text stored is not checked. A real subreddit that merely had a quiet week is not reported this way.
- **A subreddit that returns nothing is not marked "Last synced".** A misspelt subreddit name (Arctic Shift just answers with nothing) used to show a fresh "Last synced" time and 0 threads, as if it had worked. If a sync reads nothing and nothing is stored for the subreddit, the time stays "never".
- **Community sync says why it added nothing.** A sync that found nothing new used to print "0 new threads and 0 new comments", whether the week was already stored or Arctic Shift returned nothing at all. It now says "Already up to date: all N threads and M comments it found were already stored" in the first case, and "Arctic Shift returned no threads or comments for the last 7 days" (with what to check) in the second.
- **Remove books from a category, or from the library.** *Skip* only hid a book from the queue, so a wrong-genre book on a Best Sellers list still counted in the category's analyses, searches and the MCP. The Capture queue now has **Remove from category** per book: it is taken out of that category's lists for good (it stays out when the list is captured again and after a rebuild, and is remembered in `user/user-data.json`), and a **Removed from categories** table under the queue lets you **Restore** it. A captured book stays in the library. A book's page has a **Remove** section: **Remove from <category>** for each list it is on, **Remove from library…** (deletes its pages, cover, text, notes and files; a rebuild does not bring it back) and **Remove from library and all categories…**. Removing a book also clears what was derived from it (cover measurements, search-index entries), and the open window reloads its analyses; the analyses themselves are computed from the library each time, so they update at once. Category search no longer counts a removed book that the book's own page ranks in the category. The MCP has `exclude_from_category`, `restore_to_category` and `remove_book` (needs `confirm: true`), all marked as changing the library.
- **Keyword suggestions no longer repeat what Amazon already reads, and respect the readership.** Suggest Keywords used to offer the category name and then a trope with the category stuck in front ("cozy fantasy found family"). Suggestions now leave out every word of the category (name and path), so they are the trope or title phrase on its own, and the category itself is no longer suggested. A new **Avoid words in** drop-down (your books that have a captured page) also leaves out that book's title, subtitle and blurb words. A phrase with nothing left after that is dropped.
- **"Reverse harem / why choose" is women's romance, and plain "harem" is not.** The two were one trope, so harem fantasy and LitRPG (a male lead with many women) were counted as "reverse harem / why choose" in the Reader Community analysis, in character reading and in the suggestions. "Harem" is now its own trope, and "reverse harem / why choose" needs "reverse harem" or "why choose" in the text. Suggestions also skip "why choose" for a category whose name or linked subreddits are aimed at men (men's, LitRPG, progression, harem, military, westerns).
- **Keywords: Suggest Keywords marks which phrases autocomplete knows.** From the answers already saved (no request is made), a suggested phrase shows ✓ when an autocomplete source has suggested it, ⚠ when every source asked had no suggestions for it (too specific; use Search on Amazon to see its results), and no mark when it has not been looked up yet. The marks update after each lookup.
- **Keywords: a phrase with no autocomplete suggestions is explained, and falls back to a shorter one.** Autocomplete only lists phrases many people type, so a very specific phrase such as "cozy fantasy dungeon" can get nothing from every source. Lookup now says "answered, but has no suggestions for this phrase" instead of a bare "0 phrases"; when every source is empty it retries with the last word dropped (never below two words), shows that broader phrase's results with a note, and offers a button to search the phrase you asked about on Amazon. An empty answer is also no longer saved for a week: it is asked again after ten minutes, or at once with Refresh.
- **"Search on Amazon" is captured automatically.** The Amazon search that Keywords opens (the button above the table, or **Amazon ↗** beside a phrase) is tagged with a fragment Amazon never sees; the extension captures a tagged search page as soon as it finishes loading, so the Amazon results, ratings and sales columns fill in without clicking the extension. Searches you open yourself are captured with the button as before. Reload the extension (chrome://extensions) to pick this up.

## 0.4.1

- **Fix: the Claude Desktop extension (BookScout.mcpb) failing with "BookScout couldn't start".** Claude Desktop gives a new server only a fraction of a second to answer its first message, and BookScout's server (the app's runtime loading your library) takes a few hundred milliseconds on a fast computer and longer on others, so Claude dropped it. The extension's launcher now starts the server immediately, answers Claude's handshake itself at once (from what the server reports about itself, so the two cannot drift apart), holds Claude's next messages until the server is ready and then passes everything through. If the server dies, the waiting requests get an error that says why, and what happened is written to Claude Desktop's log and to `logs\mcp-launcher.log` in BookScout's settings folder. Install the extension again from Settings to get the new launcher.
- **Install in Claude Desktop no longer tries to open the file.** On computers where `.mcpb` is not registered to Claude Desktop, opening it offered a list of programs, and choosing Claude attached the file to a chat instead of installing it. The button now creates `BookScout.mcpb`, shows it in its folder and lists the steps (Claude Desktop → Settings → Extensions, drag the file in, click Install, restart).

## 0.4.0

- **Analysis → Reader Community.** The Reddit text you sync is now analysed, not only searchable. For the chosen category's subreddits it shows: **what readers are asking for** (the threads where someone asks for a book, best-engaged first, and the tropes and wants those requests name); **what is being discussed** (phrases that recur across threads, with how many threads and requests they appear in, average points and an example thread you can open); **what readers talk about** (humour, pacing, characters and similar themes by share of threads); **loved, avoided, and what the books deliver** (for each trope or want: threads mentioning it, requests asking for it, posts and comments that praise it or complain about it, and how many of the books on the category's lists carry it, with a read of "talked about, few books" where interest outruns supply); **what readers say they avoid** (quoted clauses such as "tired of…"); and **books readers name** (the category's listed books whose titles appear in the conversation, replies in request threads first). Everything is counted from words, not understood, and the panel opens with the caveats: a mention is not approval, praise and complaint are judged a sentence at a time, and the sample is one week per sync from the people who chose to post. The category's own words (and the subreddits' names) are left out of the topics.
- **My Books has the Library's columns.** Title, Author, Rank, Store rank, Rating, Ratings, Price, Captures and Text, as in All books, with **Pen Name** (the author name KDP reports the book under, or the captured page's first author for a book that is not in a report) where Mine was, then **Orders** (paid units) and **KU pages read** from the imported reports. Every column sorts and filters; Pen Name filters from a drop-down of your pen names.
- **Collaborations show every author, and only authors.** The Author column in All books and My Books, the book page, category reports and exports, and the MCP book tools now list everyone Amazon credits as an author on the captured page, in order, instead of the first name or everyone credited. Editors, illustrators, narrators, forewords and any other role are left out; a person credited as "(Author, Illustrator)" counts as an author. **Pen Name** in My Books lists every name a KDP report gives for a book ("A & B", "A and B", "A; B" and "A, B" are split; "Last, First" stays one name), falling back to the captured authors for a book not in a report, and its drop-down matches a book by any of its names, so picking one pen name finds the collaborations it appears in.
- **Shelf names link to Amazon.** In the Sub-category shelves table (and in the `find_subcategories` MCP result, as `url`) each shelf is a link that opens that category's Best Sellers page on Amazon. The address is built the way captured book pages link to it (the store's segment, `digital-text` for Kindle or `books`, and the browse node id, without the tracking that follows), and a shelf of an unknown store opens its browse-node page. The app builds it from the stored category, so only known shelves open.
- **Keywords: Suggest Keywords and Search History.** Above the search, **Suggest Keywords** has a drop-down of the categories you have captured and five phrases worth searching for the chosen one; click a phrase to run that search. The suggestions come from what the library already holds, so none is a request: the category itself ("cozy fantasy", a one-word category being named with its parent), then the tropes the listed books' titles and blurbs carry ("cozy fantasy found family"), phrases that recur in the listed titles, and, where Reddit text is synced, the tropes readers ask for (those come first, with the reason shown on hover and beneath). The Keywords tab now has two sub-tabs: **Search**, and **Search History**, which lists every phrase you have searched (once each, newest first, with how many times, how many phrases it turned up, which sources answered, and a Remove button, plus Clear history). Click a past search to go back to the Search page with its results, shown from the saved answers with no new request (press Refresh to ask again; if the saved answers have expired it says so). A search that found nothing anywhere is not kept.
- **MCP: `community_insights`** returns the same analysis for Claude or GPT (pair it with `community_threads` and `search_text` to read the evidence).
- Reddit thread addresses from the analysis open in your browser; the app only opens reddit.com thread addresses.

Fixes:

- **Community sync stopping part-way with "Arctic Shift rejected the query (422: Timeout. Maybe slow down a bit)".** A day's threads and comments are read in windows, and a window the service times out on is split in half down to an hour. At that size BookScout used to give up after three tries two and four seconds apart, even though the service's timeout is a load message that usually clears within a minute or two. A window that cannot be split any further is now retried through longer waits (10, 30 and then up to three of 60 seconds, about four minutes in all) before the sync stops, and after any timeout requests are spaced further apart (up to 5 seconds, easing back after a run of good answers). While it waits, the sync's step line says so ("Arctic Shift is busy: waiting 30 s, then trying again"); **Stop** still ends it at once, and everything already read stays saved.

## 0.3.0

Keyword research, sales estimates calibrated to your own KDP reports, and My Books.

- **MCP: `my_books`.** Lists the user's own books (Library → My Books) for Claude or GPT: capture status, Kindle Store rank, ratings, price, paid units and Kindle Unlimited pages read from the imported KDP reports, first and last report day, and whether sales estimates are calibrated. Filter to captured or not-captured books, sort by rank, paid units, KU pages, last sale or title. Its figures come from the user's reports, not estimates; titles are marked as untrusted text like the rest.
- **My Books.** Library has two sub-tabs, *All books* and *My Books*. Importing KDP **Orders** and **KENP Read** reports (several files at once, from My Books or from Settings → Sales estimates) creates your books from the titles, authors and ASINs the reports name, flagged as yours, even though no page of them has been captured. My Books lists each with its capture status, store rank, ratings, price, paid units, Kindle Unlimited pages read and last sale. Open a book to get an **Open on Amazon** button and the three steps to bring it into the library (open it, right-click → Send to BookScout, and it fills in with rank, price, reviews and cover); *Open next not captured* walks through them. You can also add a book by ASIN, mark any captured book as yours from its page (☆ This is my book), or remove one from My Books (imported sales and captured pages are kept; importing a report again adds it back). All books gets a ★ *Mine* column. The list is rebuilt from the saved report files and `user/user-data.json` if the database is lost.
- **Sales estimates are ranges, and can be calibrated to your own sales.** There is no official rank-to-sales conversion and published rules disagree by a factor of two or more (at rank 1,000: about 57, 116 and 148 sales a day). Estimates on the Keywords tab and from the `estimate_sales` MCP tool now show a low–high range spanning three published methods (BookScout's default, Automateed's power-law fit, Bookwriter's point estimates). Under Settings > Sales estimates, import the Orders report you download from KDP Reports (.xlsx or .csv) and BookScout pairs each rank reading from a book page you captured (or an imported rank history) with your average paid units per day over the three days before, fits `sales/day = a × rank^-b` in log space, and shows it against the published methods before you apply it. Once applied, every estimate uses your curve and its range is your fit ×/÷ the scatter of your own readings. With fewer than 4 readings, or ranks within a factor of 4 of each other, only the level is fitted and the default slope is kept; an implausible slope (outside 0.5–1.3) is rejected. Imported files are kept under `sales/` and re-read if the database is lost. The KDP download layout is not documented and was matched from KDP's help pages, not a real file: a file that cannot be read says which headers it found. 
- **Kindle Unlimited is part of the calibration.** KDP reports pages read (the **KENP Read** report), not borrows, so import it beside the Orders report (separately, or as one workbook). Borrows are estimated as pages read ÷ the book's page count in KENP (KENPC; typed per book in Settings, defaulting to the captured print length and labelled as a stand-in) ÷ the share of a book the average borrower reads (100% by default, which gives the fewest borrows the pages could mean; editable). With "Count Kindle Unlimited borrows as sales" on (the default once KENP data is imported) each rank reading is compared with paid units plus estimated borrows over the three days before, and the fitted curve and every estimate from it mean *sales plus borrows per day*, which is what moves a book's rank; the Keywords tooltip and the `estimate_sales` result say so. The comparison table shows the paid-only fit and the sales + borrows fit side by side so the effect is visible. A book with pages read but no page count is left out of the borrow fit and listed; an author with no paid orders at all can import only the KENP report (paid units are then taken as zero, and the panel says so). Amazon does not document whether borrows count like sales for rank (older author reports say they do), and the KDP KENP download layout was matched from KDP's help pages, not a real file.
- **Keywords tab.** Type a phrase and press *Look up*: BookScout asks Amazon's Kindle-store search box, Google and Bing what readers type after it (the phrase plus each letter a-z, 27 small requests per source) and lines the answers up, one column per source. Alongside them: **Reddit** mentions of the phrase in the subreddits you synced (counted locally, no request), **Google Trends** interest from a CSV you download there and import here (BookScout opens the comparison in your browser; it never calls Trends), and **Amazon competition** from search pages you capture with the extension (result count, median ratings of the first ten organic results, and estimated sales from the store ranks of results whose book pages you captured). Nothing is added into a single score and none of it is a search volume: autocomplete gives a position, Reddit a mention count, Trends a relative index.
- **Gentle by design.** Lookups run only when you press the button, from your computer, 400 ms apart, and answers are saved for 7 days, so repeating a phrase makes no request. A source that answers with a block (HTTP 429/403, or a page that is not data) is dropped after the second one and left alone for ten minutes; the result says so. Each source can be switched off in Settings.
- **The extension captures Amazon search pages** (`amazon.com/s?k=…`) as a third page kind, kept under `searches/` in the library and rebuilt with the rest. The search parser follows the markup Amazon's results are known to use and is tested on a synthetic page; if "Parser health" shows search pages with missing fields, capture a real page and the parser can be tuned against it.
- The autocomplete endpoints are undocumented and may change or be refused without notice; Amazon's terms limit automated access, which is why nothing runs unless you ask.

Fixes:

- **Fix: community sync discarding good data and failing with "rejected the query (200: ...)".** BookScout decided a response was a timeout by looking for the words "timeout" or "timed out" anywhere in it, so a page of 100 real comments that mentioned either was thrown away and retried until the sync gave up. It now judges only the HTTP status and the service's own `error` field.
- **Partial results are kept.** A day that fails part-way, or is stopped, now saves everything it had already read (threads and comments), reports the error in the result with the days completed, and *Resume sync* carries on from that day. Previously nothing from the failed day was stored.

## 0.2.1

- **About is a menu.** About BookScout now opens from an "About" menu beside "Window" in the application menu, instead of a tab.
- **Analysis has two sub-tabs.** *Category Analysis* holds the market side (opportunity, comparison, price, freshness, length, shelves, covers, trends, success score). *Text Analysis* holds what is read from the words (packaging, tropes in blurbs, reader voice, main character and love interests, trope clusters, inside the books).
- **Main character and love interest(s) analysis.** New Text Analysis section, also in the exported report: main character gender, love interest gender, number of love interests (none stated, one, triangle, several), dual POV, main character and love interest archetypes, and character and relationship tropes, each for the top N against the rest. It reads each book's blurb and, when there is one, its free sample or uploaded text (the first ~60,000 characters; the text outranks the blurb for gender, and a descriptor must recur in the text to count). Dual POV is also detected from chapters headed by two alternating names. A "Re-run character analysis" button re-reads everything, for example after reading a sample or attaching a book file. Everything is read by word cues, so it shows how a book is sold rather than what is in it; "not stated" is not "absent", and dual POV counts only blurbs that say so.

- **Community sources.** Under the category picker on the Analysis tab, link one or more subreddits to a category and press *Sync last 7 days*. BookScout reads the last week's threads and their comments from the Arctic Shift archive of Reddit (a public service; no Reddit account or API key) and adds them to the library. Each sync adds to what is stored: new items are added, scores are refreshed, and items that have since been removed or deleted on Reddit are dropped. Only the thread title and text, comment text, scores, post flair and dates are kept: usernames are never stored (they are requested only to skip AutoModerator and other bots, then discarded). Everything is saved as files under `community/` in the library, so rebuilding the index loses nothing. The text is searchable with the rest of the library (keyword, and by meaning once the index is built) and each result cites its thread URL. Syncing is manual and stops at 20,000 comments per subreddit (the result says so). Scores on threads under about 36 hours old are not final; the service has no uptime guarantee, so a sync can fail and be retried.
- MCP: 3 more tools (`community_sources`, `community_threads`, `community_thread`), and `search_text` takes the kinds `community_post` and `community_comment`.

## 0.2.0

Opportunity analysis: find categories and niches worth entering.

- **Rank-to-sales estimate.** A power-law curve (default ~350 sales/day at Kindle Store #100, ~57 at #1,000, ~1.5 at #100,000) turns ranks into daily sales. The curve is stored in the library and editable. It is an order-of-magnitude guess and ignores Kindle Unlimited.
- **Category opportunity score (0-100).** Blends demand (estimated sales at #20 on the list), review barrier (ratings the top books carry), openness (share of the top published in the last year) and churn (how much the top changed between captures). Every input and its basis is shown; components with no data are listed as missing and the weights re-balanced. Weights are editable. Also reports category depth: estimated Kindle Store rank and daily sales at list positions 1, 10, 20, 50 and 100.
- **Sub-category finder.** Reads the shelf ranks on captured book pages and estimates the daily sales needed to reach the top 10 of each shelf, labelled dead, niche, competitive or hot. Flags shelves without a list capture of their own as the ones to capture next.
- **Trope clusters.** Groups a category's books by what their titles and blurbs are about (tf-idf word overlap and average-linkage clustering; deterministic, no model download) and compares each group's share of the top N with its share of the rest. Groups are marked gap (common at the top, thin below), crowded, neutral or thin. Books without a detail page are grouped by title only, and the coverage is stated.
- **Desktop Analysis tab.** New Opportunity card (score, components with their basis, depth table, editable weights and sales curve), Compare categories table, Trope clusters and Sub-category shelves; all also appear in the exported report.
- **Sample-capture log.** `logs/sample-capture.log` in the app's data folder (the same folder as `bookscout.log`, opened by the existing "open logs" action; rotated at 2 MB, three old files kept) records each sample session: what the extension saw (session start, reader found or not found, page settling, page saved or already saved, upload and screenshot errors, a book tab that is not the active tab, extension restarts, finish with no pages), what the app received (page stored with size and count, requests it refused and why), and what reading made of it (confidence and characters per page, pages read poorly, errors, and whether the closing "end of sample" screen was captured). No page content is logged. The extension reports through a new `/v1/sample/log` route. Every problem shown to you (the status bar at the bottom left of the page, the toolbar button) is also logged, as are unhandled errors in the extension and page script; repeats of the same message are counted rather than listed. If BookScout is not running, nothing can be logged.
- **Fix: "exceeds capture rate" errors.** Chrome allows two screenshots a second and the sample capture could exceed that when queued ticks fired together. Screenshots are now spaced at least 0.7 s apart and pause for 2 s after Chrome refuses one. Badge updates on closed tabs no longer raise unhandled errors.
- **Persistent tab bar.** Queue, Library, Analysis and the other tabs stay at the top of the window while a page scrolls.
- **About page, with update check and in-app update.** Shows the version and where settings, logs and the library are. "Check for updates" asks GitHub for the latest release; "Update now" downloads `BookScout-Setup-<version>.exe` from it, checks its size and the SHA-256 GitHub lists, then starts the installer and closes BookScout (your library and settings are kept). Because the repository is private, a read-only GitHub token (fine-grained, "Contents: read" on this repository) must be saved on the About page; it is stored encrypted by the operating system and sent only to github.com, or can be supplied as `BOOKSCOUT_GITHUB_TOKEN`. The installer is not code-signed, so the checksum shows the file arrived intact, not who built it. Checks and downloads are logged in `logs/updates.log`. Updating in place works in the installed Windows app; elsewhere the page offers the release link.
- MCP: 5 more tools (`category_opportunity`, `rank_categories`, `find_subcategories`, `estimate_sales`, `trope_clusters`).

Known limits: sales figures come from an approximate curve; clusters from word overlap can mislabel themes; shelf estimates rest on however many book pages you have captured.

## 0.1.0

First release.

- Chrome extension: right-click capture of Amazon (amazon.com) Kindle bestseller lists and book pages, with cover images; pairing by 6-digit code; assisted capture of the free sample.
- Windows desktop app: stores raw pages after removing personal details, parses everything on the page, keeps rank/price/review history; capture queue with "Open next"; library browser; parser-health view with "Re-read all pages".
- Text: attach a PDF, EPUB, Word or text file to a book, or read the free sample by OCR; content metrics (dialogue, sentences, reading level, openings).
- Analysis per category: market shape, packaging, tropes, reader themes, covers, success score with editable weights; CSV and report export.
- Claude / GPT integration: local MCP server (25 tools), hybrid keyword + meaning search, copy-paste setup for Claude Desktop, Claude Code and Codex.
- Library care: verify, back up, move; rotating log; tray and start-with-Windows options.

Known limits: installer is unsigned; extension is sideloaded; OCR on Amazon's real reader, the real embedding model and live amazon.com pages were not tested; scanned PDFs are not read. See `docs/plan.md`.
